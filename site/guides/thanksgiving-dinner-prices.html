Price your Thanksgiving dinner before you shop

Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26. The list is long, the turkey ads are loud, and the store with the cheapest bird is not always the cheapest for everything else. Here is how to price the whole list at the stores near you, and which lines to read twice.

Price the whole list, not just the turkey

The turkey gets the headlines, but it is one line on a list of fifteen or twenty. A store with a low turkey price can still be the dearer trip once the potatoes, butter, cream and broth are added up. So price everything at once, at every store near you, and then look at the totals.

Add sizes, so the totals mean something

A list item with no size is priced at a typical package size from the results, and the plan says so next to each cost. That is fine for celery. It is not fine for the things you buy a lot of. Write the amount you will actually buy:

  • whole turkey 14 lb. Turkeys are sold by the pound, so without a weight the plan has nothing sensible to multiply.
  • russet potatoes 5 lb, chicken broth 32 fl oz, heavy cream 1 pt, butter 1 lb.
  • Use the quantity box for multiples: two cans of cranberry sauce is quantity 2, not a new line.

Cartworth then prices exactly that amount at each store, in the same unit, so a 3 lb bag and a 10 lb bag are compared fairly.

Read the turkey deal’s conditions

The lowest turkey prices of the year usually come with strings attached. Before you plan around one, check:

  • A minimum purchase. A low or free turkey is often tied to spending a set amount in the same transaction. If you would not spend that there anyway, the deal costs more than it looks.
  • A loyalty card or digital coupon. Some prices need a card, or a coupon clipped in the store’s app before you shop.
  • A limit. Usually one bird per household or per transaction.
  • Which turkeys it covers. Frozen store brand, a named brand, fresh, or a weight range. The headline price rarely covers all of them.

Cartworth shows these conditions with each weekly-ad deal, on the Sales tab and next to the matching search results, and keeps the price without the coupon visible too.

The unit-price traps on a Thanksgiving list

The same few labels catch people every year. The unit-price guide has all six; these are the ones this list runs into:

  • Dinner rolls listed as “12 ct, 15 oz” are twelve rolls weighing 15 oz in total, not twelve 15 oz packs.
  • Broth is sold by fluid ounce, but condensed soup for a casserole is sold by weight. Compare each only with its own kind.
  • Potatoes and onions are sold by the bag in one store and by the pound, or each, in the next. Pricing loose produce “each” means assuming a weight, so treat that number as an estimate.
  • Pumpkin puree comes in more than one can size. The bigger can is not always the better buy per ounce.

When to shop

  • Look at next week’s ad early. Many stores post the next circular a few days before it starts. The web app shows next week’s ads as soon as they appear: Sales, then Next week.
  • Leave time to thaw a frozen bird. The USDA’s guidance is about a day in the refrigerator for every 4 to 5 pounds, so a large frozen turkey has to be bought, and moved to the fridge, days before Thursday.
  • Re-check before you go. Prices change during the week. Press Fresh prices on the trip plan the day you shop.

Shelf price or online price

Many stores publish their prices only through a delivery storefront, and those can run above the shelf (what we measured). Cartworth labels every price as a shelf, online, chain-wide or weekly-ad price, and the trip plan says which columns are online prices. If you are shopping in person, keep that in mind before you add a second stop.
