# Press pitch: consumer desks that run a "what will Thanksgiving dinner cost" story

**Status: draft. Nothing sent, no form filled in.** Each submission is the owner's action, or goes
out only with the owner's approval of that one outlet and that exact text.

## The angle

Every November, local TV and newspapers report the American Farm Bureau Federation's national
dinner-cost survey (in 2025: $55.18 for ten people, down $2.90, released about a week before the
holiday) and then look for a local angle. The national average says nothing about the stores a
viewer can drive to. Cartworth gives a reporter that local angle for free, in their own ZIP, in a
few minutes, with every price labelled by type. The pitch offers the tool and the method, not a
finished finding.

What the pitch must not do: hand over a "cheapest store in [city]" claim, a savings figure, or a
table of named stores and prices (`compliance.md` rules 12, 14, 21 and 34; rule 21 says a lawyer
reviews any full-dinner basket that names retailers). The reporter runs their own ZIP and reports
what they see; that is their journalism and their sourcing.

## Pitch (for a tip form or newsroom contact page; about 260 words)

**Subject / headline field:**

```
Local angle for your Thanksgiving dinner cost story: a free tool that prices the list at stores near any ZIP
```

**Message:**

```
Hello,

I'm Nick Soderstrom, a Chicago-area developer. I make Cartworth, a free website that compares grocery prices at the stores near a ZIP code. I'm writing because the national Thanksgiving dinner cost survey comes out next week, and this might help with the local half of that story.

At app.cartworth.app (no account, no sign-up) you can enter a [CITY] ZIP, add the Thanksgiving list, and see what each item costs at the stores nearby, converted to the same unit. It labels every price as a shelf price, an online listing, a chain-wide price or a weekly-ad price, because those aren't the same number. It also shows each turkey deal's conditions: minimum purchase, store card, limit one.

Two things from my own testing this month that may be useful:

- On 3 November I ran "whole turkey" in 12 metro areas. Leaving out chain-wide prices, the lowest price per pound it showed ranged from $0.79 to $2.69 depending on the city. [In CITY it was $X.XX/lb, an online listing.]
- The lowest turkey prices in the weekly ads nearly always had strings: a $25 or $50 minimum purchase, a store card, or a limit of one.

I should be clear about limits. These are the prices retailers publish online, which can differ from the shelf and can be wrong. Cartworth doesn't claim to know what anyone will save, and I'm not affiliated with any store.

I'm glad to walk a reporter through it or run any ZIP on request.

Nick Soderstrom
cartworth.app
[phone or email the owner chooses to give]
```

Notes on the brackets:

- **[CITY] and the bracketed sentence:** include the city figure only for the metros in
  `facts.md` (table under "About turkey at $0.84/lb"), only with its label, and only after
  re-running that ZIP within 24 hours. Chicago is not in that table; the Chicago figure is
  `facts.md` #1 ("from $0.99 to $3.29 per pound at the eight stores that had one, 3 Nov"). For any
  other city, delete the bracketed sentence.
- **"comes out next week":** true if sent 10 to 14 Nov and the 2026 survey follows the usual
  timing (2025's came out 19 to 20 Nov). If it is already out, say "came out this week".
- **Contact line:** the owner decides what to give. Nothing personal is filled in here.

## When

Send to the Chicago desks by **Thu 12 Nov**. Thanksgiving-cost segments are usually planned the
week before the survey and air between the survey's release and the Tuesday before the holiday.
Other markets by **Mon 16 Nov**. After Fri 20 Nov it is too late for this year.

## If a reporter replies

- Offer to run their ZIP live with them, on the web app, on their screen.
- Say which prices are online listings before they ask.
- If they want a quote about savings: "It depends on what you buy and where you live. I can show
  you what each store is publishing today; I can't tell you what you'll save."
- If they ask where the prices come from: the answer on the home page, word for word.
- If they want named-store prices on air, that is their reporting from what they see on screen.
  Don't supply a prepared named-store table (`compliance.md` rule 34), and tell them published
  prices should be checked against the shelf before broadcast.
- Don't comment on any store's pricing practices.
